01 • The Problem

Cooling buildings uses energy

Hot weather creates a difficult balance: people need comfortable indoor spaces, but mechanical cooling requires electricity. Can building design reduce how much cooling is needed?

☀️

Solar heat

Sunlight heats roofs, walls and windows.

🏢

Stored heat

Materials can absorb and release heat later.

🌬️

Poor airflow

Warm air can remain trapped indoors.

Question: Can a building reduce cooling demand by responding intelligently to sunlight, airflow and temperature?
02 • Heat Sources

Tap a heat source

Click each tab to explore one source of indoor heat.

☀️ Solar gain

Sunlight heats external surfaces and can enter through windows. Shading and reflective surfaces can reduce unwanted solar heat.

🪟 Windows

Large windows can admit sunlight. External shading and suitable glazing can reduce solar heat gain.

🧱 Walls and roof

Heat can move through the building envelope. Insulation, reflective surfaces and thermal-mass strategies can alter that flow.

👥 Internal heat

People, computers, lights and appliances also release heat. Efficient equipment can reduce unnecessary internal heat.

03 • Existing Solutions

Nature already gives us cooling strategies

The innovation does not need to invent every component. The challenge is combining passive strategies into an affordable, responsive system.

🌬️

Natural ventilation

Use outdoor airflow when conditions are suitable.

🌳

Shade

Reduce direct solar heating around the building.

🧱

Thermal mass

Delay indoor temperature changes with suitable materials.

☀️

Reflective surfaces

Reduce solar radiation absorbed by surfaces.

🪟

Window shading

Control sunlight before it enters.

🧠

Smart controls

Choose suitable low-energy strategies.

04 • Unsolved Challenge

Why not simply use all of these?

The hard part is making them work together under changing weather, climate, building design and comfort requirements.

Layer Overview

🌳 External Shading

Blocks direct solar radiation prior to reaching windows and exterior walls.

Impact: Zero energy consumption. Prevents up to 70% of direct solar heat gain.

⚡ Energy Savings

Minimizes reliance on compressor-driven cooling.

🌍 Carbon Impact

Reduces peak grid loads during hot afternoon hours.

💰 Cost Balance

Passive components pay off quickly via lowered utility bills.

11 • Final Idea

Buildings that work with the climate, not against it.

“What if the smartest cooling system is the building itself?”

COOL-SKIN combines passive cooling, smart controls and efficient mechanical cooling instead of relying on one solution.

🌿🏢

Final takeaway

✓ Reduce heat before trying to remove it.
✓ Use natural conditions when suitable.
✓ Automate decisions where useful.
✓ Use AC when other strategies are insufficient.
✓ Test cost, comfort and energy together.
The innovation challenge is making this integrated approach affordable, reliable and suitable for different climates.
